Child Labour – A Serious Problem

Child labour happens when children work for money. In the past, children always helped their families, but child labour became a big problem during the Industrial Revolution in 18th century England.

Child Labour in the Past

During the Industrial Revolution, many young children worked in factories and mines. They worked long hours for very little money. Factories were dirty and had bad air. In mines, children had to crawl through small tunnels because adults could not fit inside. The work was dangerous and often made children sick.

Most children had to work because their families were poor and needed money to survive.

In the 19th century, things started to improve. More children went to school and had better lives. The famous English writer Charles Dickens wrote "Oliver Twist", a book that showed how factory work hurt children.

Child Labour Today

Even though many countries have laws that forbid child labour, millions of children still have to work. This happens mostly in Asia, Africa, and Latin America.

Many children work in factories, farms, and on the streets. Some sell things, work as tour guides, or beg for money.

According to the United Nations, there are about 200 million child workers worldwide. Of these, about 100 million work in dangerous jobs. Every year, over 22,000 children die in work accidents.

Child Labour in Agriculture

Many children in poor countries work on farms. In Brazil, for example, children as young as four years old work on sugar plantations.

Other children live in slums and work on city streets doing small jobs, such as polishing shoes, washing cars, or carrying luggage.

The Worst Forms of Child Labour

Some children are forced to do terrible jobs. Some are forced into prostitution, while others are made to fight in wars.

During the 1980s, for example, thousands of children in Iran were used as soldiers in the war against Iraq.

Child labour is still a big problem in many countries. While life has improved for some children, millions still work in dangerous conditions. Governments and organizations are working to stop child labour and give children a better future.

---

Fact: In some poor countries, children as young as 4 years old are forced to work in dangerous conditions, such as sugar plantations in Brazil.

Refugees – People Forced to Leave Their Homes

Rafugees are people who leave their country because of wars, political problems, or religious conflicts. They run away because they fear for their lives or face danger from their government. Some people lose their homes but stay in their own country. They people are called displaced persons.

How Many Refugees Are Their Today?

There are about 12 million refugees in the world today. The highest number was during the Balkan conflict in the 1990s, when there were 18 million refugees.

Over 800,000 people become refugees every year, mostly escaping wars and conflicts in Africa and the Middle East. In 2011, thousands of people fled from North African dictatorships during the Arab Spring. In 1994, hundreds of thousands of Rwandans ran away from genocide and terror.

Countries with the Most Refugees

Afghanistan has the highest number of refugees — almost 3 million. Most Afghan refugees go to Pakistan, while others have moved to Germany (over 500,000) and the United States (over 250,000).

Other countries with many refugees include:

★ Iraq and Somalia — Over 1 million refugees ★ Sudan and Congo — Over 500,000 refugees each ★ Kurds and Palestinians — Over 10 million people without a country

Not all refugees leave because of war. In the Horn of Africa, over 12 million people have lost their homes because of drought.

In total, over 40 million people are homeless worldwide due to conflicts. Many of may never be able to return home.

Who is a Refugee?

International law defines a refugee as a person who has five other homes and return for many reasons. Refugees are protected by the Geneva Convention, they cannot be forced to go back to their country.

The UNHCR (United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees) help refugee.

◆ Helping them return home if possible - Helping them stay and live in a new country - Protecting their rights

Many countries do not give refugees the same rights as citizens.

It is often difficult to separate refugees from economic immigrants. Refugees flee for safety, while economic immigrants leave to find better jobs and income.

Refugees leave their countries because of wars, political problems, or natural disasters. The United Nations and other organizations help them find safety and a better future. However, many refugees struggle to find a new home, and some may never return to their homeland.

Fact: Around 50% of refugees are children, many of whom travel without their parents.

Bolalar Mehnat — Jiddiy Muammo

Bolalar mehnat bolalar pul uchun ishlaganda sodir bo'ladi. Ilgari bolalar har doim oilalariga yordam berishgan, ammo 18-asr Angliyadagi Sanoat inqilobi davrida bolalar mehnati katta muammoga aylandi.

O'tmishdagi bolalar mehnati

Sanoat inqilobi davrida ko'plab yosh bolalar fabrikalar va konlarda ishlagan. Ular juda oz pul evaziga uzoq soatlar ishlashardi. Fabrikalar iflos va havosi yomon edi. Konlarda bolalar kichik tunnellardan emaklab o'tishga majbur bo'lishardi, chunki kattalar sig'masdi. Ish xavfli edi va ko'pincha bolalarni kasal qilar edi.

Ko'pchilik bolalar ishlashga majbur edi, chunki oilalari kambag'al edi va tirik qolish uchun pulga muhtoj edi.

19-asrda vaziyat yaxshilana boshladi. Ko'proq bolalar maktabga borishdi va yaxshiroq hayot kechirishdi. Mashhur ingliz yozuvchisi Charlz Dikkens "Oliver Twist" kitobini yozgan, u zavod ishi bolalarga qanchalik zarar yetkazganini ko'rsatgan.

Bugungi kunda bolalar mehnati

Garchi ko'plab mamlakatlarda bolalar mehnatini taqiqlovchi qonunlar mavjud bo'lsa-da, millionlab bolalar hali ham ishlashga majbur. Bu ko'pincha Osiyo, Afrika va Lotin Amerikasida sodir bo'ladi.

Ko'pgina bolalar fabrikalarda, fermer xo'jaliklarida va ko'chalarda ishlaydi. Ba'zilar narsalar sotadi, gid bo'lib ishlaydi yoki pul so'raydi.

Birlashgan Millatlar Tashkilotining ma'lumotlariga ko'ra, butun dunyo bo'ylab 200 millionga yaqin bola ishchi bor. Ulardan 100 millionga yaqini xavfli ishlarda ishlaydi. Har yili 22,000 dan ortiq bola ish joyidagi baxtsiz hodisalarda vafot etadi.

Qishloq xo'jaligida bolalar mehnati

Kambag'al mamlakatlardagi ko'plab bolalar fermalarda ishlaydi. Masalan, Braziliyada to'rt yoshgacha bo'lgan bolalar shakar plantatsiyalarida ishlaydi.

Boshqa bolalar esa gettolarda yashab, shahar ko'chalarida poyabzal tozalash, mashina yuvish yoki yuk ko'tarish kabi kichik ishlarni bajarishadi.

Bolalar mehnatining eng yomon shakllari

Ba'zi bolalar dahshatli ishlarni qilishga majbur bo'ladi. Ba'zilar fohishalikka majburlanadi, boshqalari esa urushlarda jang qilishga majbur bo'ladi.

Masalan, 1980-yillarda Eronlik minglab bolalar Iroqqa qarshi urushda askar sifatida ishlatilgan.

Bolalar mehnati ko'plab mamlakatlarda hali ham katta muammo bo'lib qolmoqda. Ba'zi bolalar uchun hayot yaxshilangan bo'lsa-da, millionlab bolalar hali ham xavfli sharoitlarda ishlamoqda. Hukumatlar va tashkilotlar bolalar mehnatini to'xtatish va bolalarga yaxshiroq kelajak berish uchun ishlamoqda.

Qochqinlar - Uylarini Tark Etishga Majbur Bo'lgan Odamlar

Qochqinlar - bu urushlar, siyosiy muammolar yoki diniy nizolar tufayli o'z vatanini tark etgan odamlar. Ular o'z hayotlari uchun qo'rqqanlari yoki hukumatlari tomonidan xavfga duch kelganliklari sababli qochib ketishadi. Ba'zi odamlar uylarini yo'qotadi, ammo o'z vatanlarida qolishadi. Bu odamlar majburiy ko'chirilgan shaxslar deb ataladi.

Bugungi kunda qochqinlar soni qancha?

Bugungi kunda dunyoda 12 millionga yaqin qochqin bor. Eng yuqori ko'rsatkich 1990-yillarda Bolqon mojarosi davrida bo'lgan, o'shanda 18 million qochqin bo'lgan.

Har yili 800,000 dan ortiq odam qochqinga aylanadi, ular asosan Afrika va Yaqin Sharqdagi urushlar va mojarolardan qochishadi. 2011-yilda minglab odamlar Arab bahori davrida Shimoliy Afrikadagi diktaturalardan qochib ketishdi. 1994-yilda yuz minglab Ruandaliklar genotsid va terrorchilikdan qochishgan.

Eng ko'p qochqinlarga ega mamlakatlar

Afg'oniston eng ko'p qochqinlar soniga ega - deyarli 3 million. Aksariyat afg'on qochqinlari Pokistonga boradi, boshqalari esa Germaniyaga (500,000 dan ortiq) va AQShga (250,000 dan ortiq) ko'chib o'tgan.

Ko'plab qochqinlarga ega bo'lgan boshqa mamlakatlar qatoriga quyidagilar kiradi:

★ Iroq va Somali - 1 milliondan ortiq qochqin ★ Sudan va Kongo - har birida 500,000 dan ortiq qochqin ★ Kurdlar va Falastinliklar - mamlakati bo'lmagan 10 milliondan ortiq odam

Barcha qochqinlar urush tufayli ketmaydi. Afrika shoxida qurg'oqchilik tufayli 12 milliondan ortiq odam o'z uylarini yo'qotgan.

Umuman olganda, butun dunyo bo'ylab 40 milliondan ortiq odam mojarolar tufayli uysiz qolgan. Ularning ko'pchiligi hech qachon uylariga qayta olmasligi mumkin.

Qochqin kim?

Xalqaro qonunchilik qochqinni beshta boshqa uyi bor va ko'plab sabablarga ko'ra qaytib kela olmaydigan shaxs sifatida belgilaydi. Qochqinlar Jeneva konventsiyasi bilan himoyalangan, ular o'z mamlakatlariga qaytishga majburlana olmaydi.

BMT Qochqinlar bo'yicha Oliy Komissarligi (UNHCR) qochqinlarga yordam beradi.

◆ Agar mumkin bo'lsa, ularga uylariga qaytishga yordam berish - Ularga yangi mamlakatda qolish va yashashga yordam berish - Ularning huquqlarini himoya qilish

Ko'plab mamlakatlar qochqinlarga fuqarolarga beriladigan huquqlarni bermaydi.

Qochqinlarni iqtisodiy muhojirlardan ajratish ko'pincha qiyin. Qochqinlar xavfsizlik uchun qochadi, iqtisodiy muhojirlar esa yaxshiroq ish va daromad topish uchun ketadi.

Qochqinlar o'z vatanlarini urushlar, siyosiy muammolar yoki tabiiy ofatlar tufayli tark etishadi. Birlashgan Millatlar Tashkiloti va boshqa tashkilotlar ularga xavfsizlik va yaxshiroq kelajak topishga yordam beradi. Biroq, ko'plab qochqinlar yangi uy topishda qiynaladi va ba'zilari o'z vatanlariga hech qachon qaytmasligi mumkin.

Fakt: Qochqinlarning 50% ga yaqini bolalar, ularning ko'pchiligi ota-onalarisiz sayohat qiladi.
